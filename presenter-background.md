## Her framework: "Quality by Design"

Preventing errors rather than catching them later. Two principles: (1) **upstream prevention over downstream correction** — validation rules in source systems, real-time validation at point of entry, automated checks at every system-to-system transfer; (2) **systematic risk management** — map data flows for failure points, automated anomaly alerts, pre-defined escalation procedures, test submission processes well before deadlines. Her analogy: "like clinical trial database locks — you don't wait until the day before to check if your data is clean."

## Leadership philosophy

Clear accountability with collaborative ownership; psychological safety as a driver of *better outcomes*, not just morale (junior staff caught process improvements senior staff missed); people perform best understanding *why*, not just *what*; influence without formal authority built through being genuinely useful, not positional power ("success comes from being genuinely helpful and reliable, not from positional authority"); service-oriented stakeholder engagement — position the team as problem-solvers, not demand-makers.

## Signature stories

**The multi-site data inconsistency fix** (behind the "vaccination dates" story already in the Video 2 script): 3 HDSS sites had drifted into different interpretations of the same collection protocol. Root cause, found via field visits and stakeholder interviews: written procedures weren't detailed enough for complex real-world scenarios. Fix: visual decision trees, site-specific training, real-time validation, live per-site dashboards. Result: 95% accuracy improvement, and field staff reported *higher* job satisfaction from immediate feedback instead of delayed criticism.

**Career pivot narrative** (source for backlog Video 7): ~9 years in software development, including founding her own healthtech company, before moving into data management. Her own words: she could write "the cleanest code you've ever seen," but if two departments define "active customer" differently, the system "faithfully stores beautiful nonsense" — the system was never the problem, the shared understanding was. (Already used verbatim in the produced Video 2 script.)

**Risk-based validation**: focuses intensive checks on high-impact variables, automated monitoring for routine ones — how she keeps multiple concurrent studies moving without compromising quality. Fits backlog #11 or #21.

**Data freeze & version control discipline**: clear data-freeze procedures and audit trails so validation happens on a stable snapshot while every change stays traceable, modelled on clinical trial database locks.

**Building trust with people who don't report to you**: won over MRCG field-site staff outside her direct authority by cutting their admin burden, training them, and publicly crediting their contributions — they went from resistant to actively seeking her input. Fits a "how to get people to give you good data" video, or backlog #15 ("who owns the data").

**Planning for scale before the crunch hits**: for HESA-style deadlines, starts preparation months ahead, cross-trains so there's no single point of failure, uses integrated schedules across overlapping projects to avoid last-minute crises.

## On equity and inclusive teams

Frames equity as strategically essential to data work, not just a values statement — diverse teams "produce better analytical insights" and catch quality issues homogeneous teams miss, which she ties directly to reporting metrics like widening-participation and graduate-outcome disparities. Practices: psychological safety, development opportunities assigned by individual goals rather than assumptions, active attention to different communication/cultural styles, and making sure diverse voices are actually in the room for strategic decisions.
