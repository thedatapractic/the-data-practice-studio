## How to read it

**Audience.** **S** = students and aspiring data professionals. **P** = practising data professionals. **Both** = genuinely serves each without weakening for either.

The honest answer to your question is **no, not every title suits both audiences, and that is fine.** A video that tries to serve both usually serves neither: the student wants orientation and the professional wants a fix, and those are different videos. What matters is the balance across the library, which is assessed at the bottom.

**Subject area.** DM = data management · DG = data governance · DA = data analytics · SP = SQL and Python. **Careers** is marked separately because it cuts across all four rather than being a subject of its own, and it is the main way the student audience is reached.

## Balance

Counting the lean rather than the ideal, after this week's three additions:

- **Students first: 10** videos (3, 4, 6, 8, 9, 13, 18, 19, 26, 32), plus 27 leaning that way *(4, 6 and 13 replace what were 17, 5 and 12 before the renumbering; the underlying set of S-leaning videos is unchanged by the renumbering itself, only by the addition of #32)*
- **Professionals first: 14** videos (2, 5, 7, 11, 12, 15, 16, 21, 23, 24, 25, 30, 31), plus 20 partly
- **Genuinely both: 8–9** videos (1, 10, 14, 17, 22, 27, 28, 29, and 20 arguably)

**Verdict: the professional tilt has widened, not narrowed, this week — say so plainly rather than softening it.** Adding #30 (data contracts) and #31 (agentic AI governance) was the right call on the evidence — both are genuinely current, well-sourced governance-maturity stories with nothing comparable already on the backlog — but both are P-leaning, and #32 was added specifically to buy back one S-leaning slot rather than let the gap widen unchecked. Net effect: three items added, the ratio still moved toward professionals. **This is worth watching on the next pass rather than immediately** — the channel is still young enough that professional-pain content bringing in the audience is defensible, per the 2026-08-03 assessment, but that defence gets weaker each time the tilt widens rather than holds. If the next weekly run finds another strong P-leaning topic, it should actively look for an S-leaning one to pair with it rather than add it alone.

The student-facing videos remain clustered in careers content (8, 9, 19, 26, and now 32) rather than spread across the subjects. A student who is not asking a careers question still has fewer entry points than a professional has pain points.

## The gaps as originally found

1. **Python is missing entirely.** You name SQL *and Python* as a subject area, and **not one video on the backlog covers Python.** For a channel that claims it, that is the biggest hole on the list. A "Python for data cleaning, the parts you actually use" video would serve both audiences and fill it.
2. **Data analytics is thin.** Only #14 sits squarely in analytics, with #18 partly *(numbering as it stood 2026-08-03, before this week's renumbering — the analytics video referred to is now #15)*. For one of your four named subject areas, that is under-served, especially given your own Power BI depth. Candidates: reading a dataset before you analyse it, and what makes an analysis trustworthy rather than just presentable.
3. **Careers is doing heavy lifting but is not one of your four subject areas.** Five videos are careers content and they carry most of the student audience. Worth deciding whether careers is formally a fifth area or stays cross-cutting. I have kept it cross-cutting for now.
