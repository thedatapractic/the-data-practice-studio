## How to read it

**Audience.** **S** = students and aspiring data professionals. **P** = practising data professionals. **Both** = genuinely serves each without weakening for either.

The honest answer to your question is **no, not every title suits both audiences, and that is fine.** A video that tries to serve both usually serves neither: the student wants orientation and the professional wants a fix, and those are different videos. What matters is the balance across the library, which is assessed at the bottom.

**Subject area.** DM = data management · DG = data governance · DA = data analytics · SP = SQL and Python. **Careers** is marked separately because it cuts across all four rather than being a subject of its own, and it is the main way the student audience is reached.

## The gaps as originally found

1. **Python is missing entirely.** You name SQL *and Python* as a subject area, and **not one video on the backlog covers Python.** For a channel that claims it, that is the biggest hole on the list. A "Python for data cleaning, the parts you actually use" video would serve both audiences and fill it.
2. **Data analytics is thin.** Only #14 sits squarely in analytics, with #18 partly. For one of your four named subject areas, that is under-served, especially given your own Power BI depth. Candidates: reading a dataset before you analyse it, and what makes an analysis trustworthy rather than just presentable.
3. **Careers is doing heavy lifting but is not one of your four subject areas.** Five videos are careers content and they carry most of the student audience. Worth deciding whether careers is formally a fifth area or stays cross-cutting. I have kept it cross-cutting for now.

## Recommendation

Approve a Python video and an analytics video to close gaps 1 and 2. Both would improve the student balance without adding more careers content, which is currently how the student audience is served almost exclusively.
