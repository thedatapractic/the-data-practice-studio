# Audience and subject map

Linda's standing assessment of who each video serves and what it teaches. Reviewed in the weekly run and updated whenever the backlog changes.

*Assessed 2026-08-03. Renumbered and reassessed 2026-08-10 to match `backlog.md`'s reordering — see that file's Notes for what moved and why. Content of each assessment carried over unchanged except where the row itself moved or a new row was added.*

## How to read it

**Audience.** **S** = students and aspiring data professionals. **P** = practising data professionals. **Both** = genuinely serves each without weakening for either.

The honest answer to your question is **no, not every title suits both audiences, and that is fine.** A video that tries to serve both usually serves neither: the student wants orientation and the professional wants a fix, and those are different videos. What matters is the balance across the library, which is assessed at the bottom.

**Subject area.** DM = data management · DG = data governance · DA = data analytics · SP = SQL and Python. **Careers** is marked separately because it cuts across all four rather than being a subject of its own, and it is the main way the student audience is reached.

**Verdict: unchanged from 2026-08-03 — reasonably balanced, tilting slightly towards professionals.** Reordering the list doesn't change which videos exist, so the balance itself hasn't moved this run; only the sequence has. The tilt is defensible while the channel is young, because professional-pain content is more shareable and brings the audience in, but it is worth watching. The student-facing videos are still clustered in careers content rather than spread across the subjects, which means a student who is not asking a careers question has fewer entry points.

This isn't a subject-area gap (identity cuts across all four, like careers does) but it is a pillar gap worth Aji's attention; see the note against #13 in `backlog.md` for the specific recommendation.
