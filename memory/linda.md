## What makes a topic land
- 2026-08-10 — **The confusion query beats the definition query, by roughly 8x.** Live YouTube check: Lights On Data's best-performing video in this niche is not a definition, it is **"What is the Difference Between Data Management and Data Governance?" at 300k views**, while the strongest result on the bare query "what is data management" is **38k**. People do not search to be given a definition, they search to get unstuck between two terms they keep hearing and cannot separate. **Prefer a title that resolves a confusion over one that defines a term**, and look for the pair of terms the audience is stuck between.
- 2026-08-10 — **A time-boxed promise in the title is doing measurable work.** IBM Technology's **"Data Governance Explained in 5 Minutes" has 353k views**; Adam Finer's "What Is Data Governance? (And Why It Matters)" has **6.6k**. Same topic, same definitional intent. Pricing the watch up front beats asking for open-ended trust. Worth testing on our search-intent titles.
- 2026-08-10 — **Evergreen compounds and recency decays, measurable on a single channel.** Every top performer found on data-management queries is **3–5 years old and still ranking**, while Lights On Data's *recent* AI-governance uploads sit at **982 to 24k views** against their 300k evergreen explainer — a gap of more than an order of magnitude on the same channel with the same audience. This is hard evidence for the existing rule that AI is **seasoning on an evergreen frame, never the frame itself**, and it is the reason to keep declining "lean into the AI wave" title alternatives.
- 2026-08-10 — **Where the volume actually is: technical skills, not concepts.** On a view-sorted search, the giants in "data" are a DBMS introduction at **4.1m** and an SQL beginners tutorial at **2.9m**. Concept, management and governance content tops out in the low hundreds of thousands. **The SQL and Python subject area has roughly ten times the demand of data-management concepts**, which should weigh in backlog sequencing, not just gap-filling.
- 2026-08-10 — **Chapters appear on every top performer** and YouTube prints them directly into search results, adding surface area and a visible promise of structure. Treat chapters as a packaging requirement, not an optional extra.
- 2026-08-03 — **Two audiences, balanced across the library rather than inside each video.** Students/aspiring professionals and practising professionals. Not every video can serve both well, and forcing it weakens the video; what matters is the balance overall. Every backlog item carries an explicit audience judgement in `audience-map.md`, which I maintain and review weekly. If the library drifts to one audience, say so and propose what to add.
- 2026-08-03 — **The channel is not only for data managers.** Subject areas are **data management, data governance, data analytics, and SQL and Python** for practical data work. Tag every item so gaps are visible. Careers content cuts across all four and is currently how almost the entire student audience is reached, which is itself a risk.
- 2026-08-03 — **Gaps found in the first audience assessment:** Python is named as a subject area and has **zero** videos; data analytics has effectively one. Watch for a named subject area with no coverage; it is the easiest gap to miss because the backlog looks full.
- 2026-08-03 — **Reordering breaks tease chains.** Moving the data-manager video to position 3 left its closing tease pointing at a video that now publishes before it. **Whenever the order changes, check every finished script's closing tease against the new sequence.**
- 2026-08-02 — **Title authority.** I may rewrite the title of any backlog item **not yet produced**. I may **never** retitle anything already produced, because those titles are live and carry the video's search history and links. Where a script exists but the video is not produced, the title also sits inside the script and on the thumbnail, so I **propose** a better title to Aji rather than changing it.
- 2026-08-02 — **Backlog curation is now my weekly job**, and it is the main thing I do unattended: add new topics marked as mine for Aji's review, reassess every existing item for whether it is still worth making and what its strongest title is now, reorder on relevance and sequencing, check for beat collisions with finished scripts, log it, and recommend what to script next. Consecutive videos drawing on the same examples makes the channel look like it has a thin bank of stories. ## Angles and hooks that worked or flopped
- 2026-08-11 — **A time-box in the title only works if the time is short. Do not recommend it for a long video.** I pushed "Explained in N Minutes" three times on the evidence of IBM's 353k against 6.6k for an untimed equivalent. The finding is real, but the mechanism is *a cheap promise*, and Video 1 runs 9:55 — advertising ten minutes on a definitional query deters rather than attracts. **Check the actual runtime before recommending a duration promise**, and reserve it for videos genuinely under about six minutes.
- 2026-08-11 — **Video 1's subtitle: "The Million-Dollar Problem Nobody Owns" → "The Job Nobody Owns"** (Aji, 2026-08-11). The lesson for future titling: **a subtitle has to be earned by the script end to end, not just be catchy.** The money version obliged the video to lead on cost, while the hook now leads on a decision nobody made; the ownership version is paid off by the Part 1 bridge, Part 3's first move and the landing homework. It also removes the title/script figure-consistency risk Dan has flagged twice, since the title no longer has to agree with the Gartner number, and **"job" reads as opportunity to the student audience** where "million-dollar problem" reads as someone else's crisis.
- 2026-08-10 — **The winning hooks in this niche validate a feeling, they do not dramatise a consequence.** All three strongest performers open on the viewer's own mental state and promise relief from it: Lights On Data on named confusion ("There's a lot of confusion between these two concepts..."), IBM Technology on a time contract (duration in the title, chapter one is simply "Intro"), Eric Kimberling on overwhelm ("full of acronyms... can seem overwhelming"). **Not one opens on business stakes, a cost figure or a dramatised consequence.** Evidence from titles, chapter-one names and description openers, not from verbatim transcripts.
- 2026-08-10 — **The gap in the market is lived practitioner stakes.** IBM is corporate, Firican is consultative, the smaller channels are classroom. Nobody in this niche has Aji's operational history. That is the channel's differentiator, but the evidence says it belongs in the **credibility beat straight after the hook**, not in the hook, where every winner stays on the viewer's confusion.
- 2026-08-02 — Refresh stale headline economics to a **current, per-organisation figure** (e.g. ~$12.9M/yr, Gartner) instead of a dated global "$3 trillion". A per-company number lands harder for a single viewer and reads as more credible.

- 2026-08-10 — **Method that works, and its limits.** Reading view counts and subscriber counts **live from YouTube in the browser** is far better evidence than the "best channels for X" listicles, which are stale and rank by opinion. Two limits to state honestly every time: the browser pane renders only the **top 2–4 results per search**, so it is the head of the result page and not an exhaustive scan; and **verbatim hooks could not be extracted** — YouTube's caption endpoint is locked down in the sandboxed browser, the on-page transcript panel would not populate, one transcript site returned 403 and another required an account. **So hook analysis in this niche is inferred from titles, chapter names and description openers. Never present it as a transcript quote.**
- 2026-08-10 — **Sort order changes what the question means.** A view-sorted search on "data governance explained" returns tangential DBMS and SQL giants; a relevance-sorted search on "what is data governance" returns the actual topical leaders. Run both, because the first tells you where the volume is and the second tells you who the competition is.
