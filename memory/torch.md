## Scripting lessons
- 2026-08-11 — **Gloss the terms the video teaches. Leave incidental ones alone. And never add words just because there is headroom.** Aji removed a gloss I put on *"Run a coverage analysis on that"* — "not necessary to add" — and she was right: I had twice written that it was **not** a comprehension blocker, then added it anyway because Dan raised it and I had 55 spare words. **Test for a gloss: will the viewer need this word afterwards, to search with or to sound credible?** Metadata, data steward, business glossary and data ethics all pass that test and are glossed; coverage analysis does not. **Headroom is banked, not budgeted** — spare runtime protects the next change that matters, it is not permission to fill.
- 2026-08-11 — **Never classify a meaning change as a dictation slip.** `final.md` listed "aren't really theirs" → "isn't really yours" as a transcription artefact; Aji confirmed **"theirs" was deliberate and it is the better word** — the claim is that *the organisation* does not own the data, not that the viewer does not. **A slip fixes grammar; a change to who or what the sentence is about is a voice choice.** Also: when Aji says she has checked a script, read that as *she has been editing it*, and confirm any unexplained change with her rather than inferring a source — I wrongly attributed two of her own edits to Dan and logged it in three memory files.
- 2026-08-10 — **A hook must close on the question the body actually answers next.** Aji's catch on Video 1: the hook opened "So, what is data management?", spent its length on what it is *not*, closed on "So whose job is it?", answered neither, and the body then doubled back to the first question. Her words: *"That sounds a bit like a contradiction. And it brings more confusion than clarity."* **One question, opened and then answered.** I had defended the mismatch by claiming the body answered it "in stages" — which is exactly her *having to argue for the connection is the tell* rule, applied to my own work.
- 2026-08-10 — **A hook built only on negation gives no clarity.** Saying what the subject is not, three times over, with no positive content, leaves the viewer with nothing. **Earn the negation with a story first**, then it can survive as a single clause ("no software was going to catch that") rather than being the whole opening.
- 2026-08-10 — **Opening on a concrete problem: Aji's own experience is one good option, and a generalised scenario is another.** Both satisfy "open on the viewer's problem" (template) and "hooks need something visible" (Dan) without attributing anything to the viewer's organisation. **Write it as a general recollection rather than a specific incident when that incident is needed later in the script**, so a story is not spent twice. Do not treat her experience as the *only* permitted source — that was my over-reading of the attribution rule, and she corrected it.
- 2026-08-10 — **Every beat must name the discipline it belongs to, or it floats.** Aji on the personal-data passage: it made an important point but *"didn't mention how it relates to the topic... It's just a standalone, vague statement that is doing nothing to add clarity."* The fix is the pattern Part 1 already uses for the vaccination story: name it. Personal data → **data ethics and data protection**, plus retention and destruction, which also call back to the canonical definition. **An emotionally strong line cannot carry a beat by itself; it needs the analysis around it.** The "own mother" line was not the problem and stayed.
- 2026-08-10 — **Count the runtime, never inherit it.** I reported Video 1's v2 at "~9.75 minutes" by doing arithmetic on `final.md`'s header, which claimed ~9.5 while the file was actually 1,398 words (9:59). The real v2 figure was 10:18, over the ceiling, and I told Aji it was inside. **Count the spoken words of the actual file before stating any runtime**, and treat a header's figure as a claim rather than evidence — including my own from a previous pass.
- 2026-08-10 — **A hook must validate a feeling AND show something visible; they are not alternatives.** Built the first v2 hook to the research finding and produced 96 words with nothing a camera could point at, pushing the first concrete image from 0:03 to 2:08. **The move that satisfies both rules: make the wrong explanation the visible thing** ("every explanation points you towards a database, or somebody in IT who will sort it out") — that is concrete, and it describes what other explanations say rather than asserting the viewer's situation, so the no-fabrication rule still holds.
- 2026-08-10 — **Hedging buys honesty and spends force.** "If you have already looked it up, you were probably given..." is safe but limp. **Convert a hedged claim about the viewer into a plain claim about the world** — "Look it up and you will get..." — which is checkable, needs no conditional, and reads with authority.
- 2026-08-10 — **Name a misconception at the altitude the body corrects it.** Hook said "something the software takes care of" (beginner's wrong model) while Part 1 said "managing data is the same thing as managing databases" (practitioner's conflation), so it read as one point made twice instead of one point sharpening. Same idea at two altitudes is the seam.
- 2026-08-10 — **Fixing altitude at the hook and the landing does not fix the middle.** Asked to make a professional script accessible to students, I opened up both ends and left Part 3 — 454 words, a third of the runtime — untouched and organisation-only. **Track altitude section by section, not just at the joints.**
- 2026-08-10 — **Do not attribute a scene to the viewer's own organisation. Storytelling itself is fine. NARROWED BY AJI 2026-08-10 after I recorded this rule far too broadly.** Her clarification, verbatim: *"When I said you shouldn't make up stuff about what the viewer may have in their organization, I didn't mean that you shouldn't make up generalized stories at all that are very likely to happen in most institutions. Only take it in the context where we have to say to the viewer that this is happening in your organization. These are the scenario we should avoid. Not to make a full ban on story telling."*
  - **What she rejected:** "Right now, in the organisation you work for... two people are arguing about a number." The defect is the **attribution** — telling the viewer this is happening to them.
  - **What is fine:** a generalised scenario very likely true across most institutions, her own recollection, or an invitation framed as imagination.
  - **The test is who the sentence says it happened to, not whether it was invented.** My first version of this rule treated invention itself as the defect, which led me to hedge with conditionals and "probably", to reach for negation instead of narrative, and to write a hook with nothing visible in it. **Generalised stories are a tool I should be using, not avoiding.**
- 2026-08-10 — **Write the hook to the video's actual question, not to the subtitle.** On the same rework Aji said her focus is *"not on 'The Million-Dollar Problem Nobody Owns' but rather on 'what is data management?'"* The searchable keyword phrase is the video's promise; the curiosity hook in the title is packaging. **The hook answers the searched question, not the clever half of the title.**
- 2026-08-10 — **Open on the viewer's confusion, not on stakes or cost.** Researched live on YouTube: every top performer on data-management queries opens by validating a feeling the viewer already has — confusion (Lights On Data, 300k), a time contract (IBM Technology, 353k), overwhelm (Kimberling). **Not one opens on business stakes, a cost figure or a dramatised consequence.** So the "the answer you were handed sounds like tidying" move is on-pattern, and the fire/insurance and million-dollar framings were off-pattern. Aji's lived operational stakes are the channel's differentiator but they belong in the **credibility beat immediately after the hook**, where nobody else in the niche can follow.
- 2026-08-03 — **Write for the audience the video is for, and state it at the top of the draft.** Take it from `audience-map.md`. It changes the writing, not just the label: a **student** has no organisation to apply this in, so define terms on first use, draw examples from learning rather than office politics, and never close on "run a project"; a **professional** has an organisation, so do not explain basics they already know and leave them something to do this week or language that wins an argument. For **both**, lead with the professional's concrete problem and let the student learn from watching it solved, and make the closing exercise work with or without a workplace.
- 2026-08-02 — **Read the chosen template's prohibitions, not just its arc.** On Video 1 I opened each thread with "The first is... The second is... The third is...", which `templates/career-thread.md` explicitly forbids in favour of chronological markers. Dan caught it. A template tells you what *not* to do as well as what to do, and the ordinals also spoiled the ending, because a framework should be assembled and then named, not announced three times before it arrives.
- 2026-08-02 — **Domain precision costs runtime, so budget for it from the first draft.** Adding proper domain language to the data management script (lifecycle and destruction, valid vs accurate, business metadata, data owner vs data steward, business glossary, personal data) added about 150 words and took it from 9.1 to the 10-minute ceiling. Do not write a script to length and then add expertise; write it expert from the start and cut elsewhere. Where a distinction needs more room than the video can spare, propose it as its own video instead of compressing it.
- 2026-08-02 — **The domain-language gap, and the fix.** Aji caught "the most expensive way to **run** anything" and expected "the most expensive way to **manage** data", because *we manage data, we do not run data*. Root cause: Torch's original definition said his job was packaging, not domain authority, so he wrote like a producer. That has been corrected: Torch now has **data management and governance as an explicit second skill**, with `domain-knowledge.md` as the reference to read before drafting. **Run the verb check on every draft**: for each verb attached to data, would a practitioner use that word for that thing?
- 2026-08-02 — **Aji's editorial preferences, confirmed on the data management script:**
  - Open with **"Let me ask you a question."** It makes the viewer brace rather than settle.
  - Keep **"But what about your data?"** followed by the concrete list: twenty years of customer records, patient histories, student records, transactions. Each item catches a different part of the audience.
  - Prefer a **direct question aimed at the viewer's own workplace** over an observation they can nod at ("So who in your organisation manages the data?" beats the furniture line).
  - Define data management with **plans, policies, procedures and practices** in it. She wants procedure and practice named explicitly, because that is what actually improves and secures data.
  - **Name the specific discipline** a story illustrates, not just the general principle. The vaccination case is a **metadata management** failure, and saying so is what makes it expert rather than explanatory.
  - Restore **"Data is like water in a river. If it is polluted at the source, everybody downstream drinks polluted water."** as written.
  - **Use parallel repetition deliberately.** "Same field. Same format. Three completely different meanings." is wanted: it rhymes, and a group of three gives the brain a pattern to hold. This is rhetoric, not fragment-stacking; the rule was revised to say so.
  - Avoid compound questions that blur two ideas. "Ask five people what counts as an active customer and you will get five answers" beats "what exactly counts as an active customer, and at what point is a student enrolled?"
- 2026-08-02 — **Stakeholder engagement belongs in the "how" of any governance topic**, inside the ownership move rather than as a separate one. Naming an owner achieves nothing if the people who create the data were never brought along, and Aji's real material here (reduce their workload, train them, credit them publicly) is stronger than any textbook version.
- 2026-08-02 — **The hook gets the most craft and is cut last.** Aji's standing rule: it decides whether anyone sees the rest. Follow the opening image through rather than dropping it (the fire that insures everything except the data), and close the hook on a question the viewer wants answered, not on a summary of the video.
- 2026-08-02 — **No stacked fragments.** Write flowing complete sentences that carry the listener forward. One deliberate fragment is fine; two or three in a row is a defect. Aji confirmed this after Dan flagged it independently.
- 2026-08-02 — **Cut by stripping whole sentences that repeat a point already made**, never by trimming words out of load-bearing ones. Draft with the ~1,000–1,120 word budget in view: the richer v3 draft came in at 9.9 minutes and needed 300 words removed.
- 2026-08-02 — **Creativity is a pass/fail item, not a bonus**, and scripts must read as a domain expert: specific, opinionated, and willing to say what other explainers skip. Details like "a database has no opinion about meaning" and "upstream checks made the team happier, not just faster" are what separate expert from generalist.
- 2026-08-02 — **Runtime is 7–8 minutes (~1,000–1,120 words), not 15.** Constitution change, agreed with Aji. Count the words before handing to Dan; do not hand over a long script expecting it to be trimmed later.
- 2026-08-02 — **Videos are standalone and topic-first.** Do not frame a script around DAMA-DMBOK or any book, do not use it as an on-camera prop, and do not require the viewer to care about a framework. Reference works can inform thinking; they never provide the structure.
- 2026-08-02 — For an explainer, the whole script answers **what it is, why we need it, how we do it**, and nothing else. Costs, statistics, and stories exist to give those three answers weight, never to become topics of their own.
- 2026-08-02 — Prefer a **picture or story over a statistics run** when making the "why" land (the hidden data factory beat worked better than a list of figures). At most two figures, sourced.
- 2026-08-02 — Watch cumulative **triads**: three moves, three questions, three sites in one script starts to read as the "lists of three" AI tell even when each is individually justified. Vary one.
- 2026-08-02 — State Aji's own results as hers ("in my own team, processing time dropped by about sixty percent"), never as a general finding.
- 2026-08-02 — For a foundations flagship, an AI-era beat works best as a **mid-video re-hook** (bad data → untrustworthy AI), not as the whole frame. Keeps the video evergreen.
