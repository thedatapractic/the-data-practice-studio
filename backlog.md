## Notes

- **TITLE QUESTION FOR AJI — #1, flagged by Linda 2026-08-10. Her decision, not Linda's**, because the script is written and the title sits inside it and on the thumbnail, even though the video is not yet produced.

  **The finding.** Live YouTube research on 2026-08-10 shows the **confusion-resolution** pattern massively outperforms the **definition** pattern on this exact subject: "What is the Difference Between Data Management and Data Governance?" has **300k views**, while the strongest bare "what is data management" video has **38k**. Separately, a **time-boxed** promise beats an open-ended one: "Data Governance Explained in 5 Minutes" has **353k** against **6.6k** for "What Is Data Governance? (And Why It Matters)".

  **What that would imply for #1**, e.g. "What Is Data Management? (And How It's Different From Data Governance)".

  **The honest problem with doing that, and why Linda does not recommend it.** A title that promises the governance distinction obliges the script to deliver it, and `final.md` **does not cover data governance at all**. The script already runs ~9.5 minutes against a 7–8 minute target and a 10-minute ceiling, so there is no room to add a distinction beat without cutting something Aji has already approved. Retitling would either break the title's promise or force surgery on a finished script.

  **Linda's recommendation: keep #1's title focused on the definition it actually delivers, and capture the comparison query with a dedicated video (#29).** That takes the higher-performing pattern without disturbing finished work, and it fits the standing rule that a beat needing its own room becomes its own video.

  **RESOLVED 2026-08-11 by Aji: the subtitle changed to "The Job Nobody Owns".** Full title: **"What Is Data Management? The Job Nobody Owns"**. Previous: "The Million-Dollar Problem Nobody Owns". The keyword phrase is untouched, as agreed.

  Why this one. It **keeps the proven "Nobody Owns" mechanic** from the earlier flagship while dropping a money claim the script's hook no longer makes — the hook now opens on a disputed figure and "the decision it depended on had never been made", not on cost. It is also the subtitle the script actually earns end to end: the bridge asks *"So who manages the data?"*, Part 3's first move is to put a name against the data, *"when the honest answer is that everybody does, what you mean is, it's nobody"*, and the landing homework asks who owns the report **by name**. Two side benefits: it removes the title/thumbnail/script **figure-consistency risk** Dan has flagged on two separate drafts, since the title no longer has to agree with the Gartner number, and **"job" reads as an opportunity to the student audience** where "million-dollar problem" reads as somebody else's crisis. The money stays where it belongs, inside Part 2 with its source and year.

That finding holds, but it does not transfer here: **the mechanism is a short promise, and this video is 9:55.** Advertising ten minutes on a definitional query is a deterrent, not an attraction. A time-box is worth testing on a genuinely short video, not on this one.
