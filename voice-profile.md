# Aji's voice profile

How Aji actually writes, derived from comparing her own final edited drafts against the agent drafts they came from. **Torch writes to this. Dan checks against it.** Every entry carries the evidence, because a rule without an example gets misapplied.

This is part of the stable constitution. It grows only through `/learn-from-final`, after Aji produces a final edit.

---

## Derived from: "What Is Data Management?" (v5 → Aji's final, 2026-08-02)

### 1. Economy. Cut the flourish, keep the point

The single strongest pattern. Aji removed almost every decorative phrase, including ones that were doing real rhythmic work. If a sentence exists to be enjoyed rather than to move the argument, she deletes it.

| Agent draft | Her edit |
|---|---|
| "The desks, the laptops, the server humming away in the cupboard nobody ever opens. Someone would write you a cheque, and you would buy it all back." | "The laptops, the desk, the server all replaceable." |
| "That factory is real and you are paying for it." | "That factory is real." |
| "That agreement is the work." / "The technology really is the easy part." | *(both cut)* |

**Rule:** write it, then ask whether the sentence advances the argument. If it only sounds good, cut it before Aji has to.

### 2. Restraint, not dramatisation

She consistently tones down emotional escalation. Her authority comes from being measured, not from turning up the volume. This matches the channel's stated delivery style: a knowledgeable colleague, not a hype performer.

| Agent draft | Her edit |
|---|---|
| "in public health a wrong answer is not a spreadsheet problem, it is a child who never got a vaccine" | "you based your conclusion on that, but in public health a wrong conclusion is costly" |
| "they were corrected in the moment rather than criticised a month later" | "corrected in the moment rather than having to redo them all over a month later" |

**Rule:** state the stakes plainly and let the listener feel them. Do not reach for the emotional maximum. The one exception she keeps is "treat every record as though it belonged to their own mother", because that is a real thing she said to a real team.

### 3. Never drop a line she has already written

She restored several lines from her earlier produced script that the rewrite had lost. She has a strong attachment to her own phrasing, and she is usually right, because those lines were written in her voice to begin with.

Lines she restored: *"So when I tell you data management matters, I'm not quoting a textbook. I have lived it."* · *"a single data error could affect a medical decision"* · *"the data we submit determines funding and regulation"* · *"in public health a wrong conclusion is costly"* · naming the departments: *"that's finance, it's people, HR, and it's buildings and equipment"*.

**Rule:** before rewriting a passage that exists in `scripts/produced/` or a previous approved draft, keep her wording as the default. If a change genuinely improves it, flag the change rather than making it silently.

### 4. Canonical definitions, word for word

She replaced the paraphrased definition with the discipline's formal one, restoring three things the paraphrase had softened.

- "the development and execution" → **"the development, execution and supervision"**
- "procedures and practices" → **"programs and practices"**
- "improve the value" → **"enhance the value"**

**Rule:** for a core definition of the discipline, use the canonical professional wording exactly. Plain language is for explaining the definition afterwards, never for the definition itself.

### 5. Statistics: attributed, dated, precise, spoken

| Agent draft | Her edit |
|---|---|
| "Gartner puts that bill at around twelve million dollars a year for the average organisation." | "According to the Gartner research from 2020 this is costing average organisation around twelve point nine million dollars a year." |

Three changes, all deliberate: **the source named inline**, **the year of the research stated**, and **the precise figure** rather than a rounded one, written out as it would be spoken.

**Rule:** every statistic gets its source and its year said aloud, uses the exact number, and is written phonetically for delivery ("twelve point nine million", not "$12.9m").

### 6. Concrete institutional anchors over abstractions

| Agent draft | Her edit |
|---|---|
| "someone who manages the money, someone who manages the people, and someone who manages the buildings" | "someone managing its money that's finance, it's people, HR, and it's buildings and equipment" |

**Rule:** name the actual function or department. "Finance" and "HR" land instantly; "someone who manages the money" makes the listener do the translation.

### 7. Connected clauses, not stacked punchy sentences

She repeatedly joined separate sentences with "but" and "and" so they flow as speech.

- "They passed every format rule and every range check. They simply were not accurate" → "They passed every format rule, and every range check **but** they were simply not accurate"
- "Validation catches data that is malformed. Only knowing what a field means catches data that is wrong." → joined with **but**

**This does not contradict the parallel-repetition rule.** She kept "Same field. Same format. Three completely different meanings." untouched, because that is deliberate rhetoric. The distinction holds: parallel and cumulative stays, everything else flows.

### 8. Questions to open sections, and explicit signposting

- "There are three moves here" → **"So how do we manage data? I'll give you three moves..."**
- "what is the actual harm?" → **"what is the consequences?"**
- "Think back to those vaccination dates" → "Think back to those vaccination dates **I mentioned earlier**"

**Rule:** open a section with the question it answers. Signpost callbacks explicitly, because a listener cannot scroll back.

### 9. Small word preferences observed

- **"our profession"** rather than "my profession" in general statements; she includes the audience.
- **"consequences"** rather than "harm".
- **"health data"** rather than "clinical data" for the general audience.
- **"a start you could make today"** rather than "on Monday".
- **"the data owner"** rather than "your data owner".
- "no **insurance** policy on earth", not "no policy on earth" — she removes ambiguity even at the cost of a word.
