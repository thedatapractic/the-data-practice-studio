# Video brief — What Is Data Management?

*Prepared by Linda, 2026-08-02. Revised the same day after Aji's constitution changes: **7–8 minute runtime**, and **standalone, topic-first** videos with no book framing. Handoff to Torch.*

## Scope for this runtime
At 7–8 minutes (~1,000–1,120 words) this video answers exactly three things: **what data management is, why we need it, and how we do it.** Everything else is cut. The knowledge-area map, the textbook framing, and the "data behaves oddly as an asset" material are **out of scope for this video** and are better as their own future videos.

## Recommended title
**What Is Data Management? The Million-Dollar Problem Nobody Owns**

*(Currency resolved 2026-08-02: title uses dollars to match the Gartner figure quoted in the script. Title, thumbnail and spoken line now agree.)*

- Keeps the proven, high-performing "nobody owns it" hook from the earlier flagship, but **updates the economics**: the dated "$3 trillion" IBM figure is now widely recognised as old. The current, more credible and more relatable number is Gartner's **~$12.9 million per organisation per year** for poor data quality. A per-company figure lands harder for a single viewer than a global trillion.
- Alternative, if we want to lean into the AI wave: **"What Is Data Management? The Hidden Reason Your AI Projects Keep Failing."** Higher ceiling on views right now, but narrower; I'd keep AI as a strong beat inside the flagship rather than the whole frame, so the video stays evergreen.

## Pillar and audience
- **Pillar 1** (data management & governance foundations). This is the channel's **search flagship / start-here** video.
- **Both segments**, primary is search-intent: career-builders searching "what is data management", and problem-holders who feel the pain but can't name it.

## Purpose (one sentence)
Make the viewer stop seeing data as an IT by-product and start seeing it as an unowned, high-value asset that must be deliberately managed, so they leave able to spot the gap in their own organisation.

## Angle / hook idea
Open on the ownership gap made vivid (the asset no insurance replaces), then escalate with the modern cost, including the 2026 twist: **bad data is now also why AI projects hallucinate and fail.** The topic is evergreen, but the AI angle makes it feel urgent in 2026.

## Why it has potential now (researched)
- **Evergreen, high-intent search term.** "What is data management" is a stable definitional query that career-builders search year-round; a strong flagship becomes a long-term traffic entry point.
- **Live confusion worth owning:** a lot of 2026 content argues "data governance vs data management" as if they're rivals. We can own the clean explanation of how they fit together, which most videos muddle. (Sources: IBM 2026 data management guide; Alation/Splunk "governance vs management" 2026 pieces.)
- **Fresh, credible economics:** poor data quality ~$12.9M per org per year (Gartner, still cited through 2026); 15–25% of revenue lost to bad data (MIT Sloan); knowledge workers lose up to ~50% of their time to bad-data busywork. (Sources: IBM "true cost of poor data quality"; Integrate.io 2026 data-quality stats; Revefi.)
- **The AI beat:** bad data now shows up as AI hallucinations and model drift, which ties an evergreen topic to the single hottest search context of 2026. (Source: Integrate.io / Actian 2026.)

*Caveat (honest): these are figures from published sources, not a verified live YouTube trend scan of competitor view counts, which I can't pull directly here. Treat the "high potential" as reasoned from search intent and topic economics, not a measured trend number.*

## Must-include points for Torch
- **What:** define data management plainly, in Aji's own words, with no framework required. Clear the big misunderstanding: managing data is not managing databases. Ground it in the vaccination-dates story.
- **Why:** make the cost felt through a picture or story rather than a run of statistics. At most two figures, sourced. Include the AI beat (bad data makes AI confidently wrong) and the ethical beat ("treat every record as if it belonged to your own mother").
- **How:** two or three practical moves the viewer could start on Monday. This section must not get squeezed; it is the payoff.
- Homework tied to the viewer's own workplace, and a problem-led tease of the next video.
- **Out:** the DAMA wheel, the textbook prop, the "I'm reading the book for you" framing, and the four-ways-data-is-strange list. Not because they're weak, but because they belong to other videos.
