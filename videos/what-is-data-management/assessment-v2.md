## Checklist

### 1. Hook — **PARTIAL.** Right angle, right compliance, missing the visible.

**What works, and I want this on the record because it is the hard part.**

It opens on the searched question in its own words: *"So, what is data management?"* The viewer who typed that phrase hears it back inside four seconds. That is the correct response to Linda's finding and to the rule that the hook answers the searched question, not the curiosity subtitle.

It validates a feeling instead of dramatising a consequence, exactly as the research says wins in this niche: *"that is not your fault, because it is what almost every explanation implies."*

**And it passes the fabrication rule cleanly — I checked this hard, as asked.** Every claim about the viewer is either conditional or hedged, and every unhedged claim is about the world rather than about them:

- *"**If** you have already looked it up, you were **probably** given an answer that sounds like tidying"* — conditional plus hedge. Does not assert that she looked it up.
- *"**And if** that left you thinking this is something the software takes care of"* — conditional again. Does not assert what she thinks.
- *"it is what almost every explanation implies"* — a claim about published explanations, defensible and checkable.
- *"people can spend years working with data and never be told what this job really is"* — a claim about people in general, and one Aji has standing to make.

Nothing here invents the viewer's situation and asserts it as fact. Compare the rejected draft, which said *"Right now, in the organisation you work for... two people are arguing about a number."* The difference is structural, not cosmetic, and this hook is the pattern to reuse.

*"None of it is wrong, and that is exactly the problem"* is the best line in the hook. A paradox is a real curiosity device and it costs ten words.

**Now the failure.** `CLAUDE.md`: *"It must open on something the viewer can see and feel, with real stakes."* There is nothing to see. Count the concrete nouns in the whole hook: "data", "software", "explanation", "job". Not one of them is a thing. The only sensory word is *"tidying"*, and that is a simile about how an explanation sounds, not an object.

This matters more than it looks, because it is compounded by two other things. First, the compliance above is achieved partly through hedging — two conditionals and a "probably" in 51 words — and hedging costs force. Second, the timeline. At 140 wpm:

## The two specific questions

### Is the hook/Part 1 overlap setup-and-payoff, or repetition?

**Setup-and-payoff in structure, repetition in delivery. Signpost it; do not cut either.**

The architecture is right and I would not change it. The hook plants the wrong model at ~0:25 and Part 1 removes it at ~1:53. That is a **72-second loop**, which is a well-sized one — long enough to create tension, short enough that the viewer still holds the question. And the order is correct per the "how this goes wrong" rule: remove the wrong model, then present the right one.

The problem is that nothing tells the listener the loop is closing. *"There is no software in it and there are no servers, because the biggest misunderstanding about our profession is that managing data is the same thing as managing databases"* is 36 words, and every one of them is new information *except the one that matters* — the viewer was told 90 seconds ago that it is not the software. So the sentence's most important beat lands as its most familiar one. That is what makes it feel like repetition when it is not.

Voice-profile rule 8 is the fix and it is a small one: *"Signpost callbacks explicitly, because a listener cannot scroll back."* Two or three words in front of *"There is no software in it"* — an acknowledgement that this is the assumption from the top of the video — converts a restatement into an arrival, and makes the hook feel like it was planted deliberately rather than said twice.

Two supporting points. The overlap is **worse than it needs to be** because the two versions of the misconception are at different altitudes ("the software" versus "managing databases") — fix that per item 3 and they read as one idea sharpening rather than two statements colliding. And **the line to signpost is [PRE-EXISTING]**, so the cleanest route is for Torch to do the work on the hook side (where the text is his) and put only the three-word signpost to Aji.

### Is *"So if it is not the software, what is it?"* a strong enough open loop, and does the body answer it?

**The body answers it well. The loop itself is adequate and correctly aimed, and it is the weakest of the hook's four beats.**

**On the answer: unambiguously yes, and twice.** The negative half closes at 1:53 (*"There is no software in it and there are no servers"*). The positive half is the strongest sentence in the script:

> *"Data management is somebody deciding what a field means, who owns the data, what good looks like and what happens when it goes wrong, and then writing those decisions down as policy and procedure so they outlive the person who made them."*

That is a direct, complete answer to "if it is not the software, what is it?" — it is a person, a decision and a written record. And the pivot into it (*"a database has no opinion about meaning"*) is the exact hinge the question needs. The loop is honoured. No concern here at all.

**On the loop's strength.** Replacing *"let me give you the answer I would have wanted when I started"* was the right call and for the right reason — it was a beginner's promise on a professionally-pitched script, and it also made the hook a promise about the *video* rather than about the *subject*, which is the weaker of the two.

But the replacement does not add tension; it re-asks the title's question in the negative. And it sits in the position of maximum emphasis, the last thing before the credibility beat. **The strongest line in the hook is already the one before it:** *"It is why people can spend years working with data and never be told what this job really is."* That has personal stakes, it works on both audiences at once (the student hears a future, the professional hears their past), and it is far more uncomfortable than "so what is it?" The hook currently peaks on its third beat and then steps down onto its fourth.

**My recommendation, and it is a small one.** Keep the shape — a question is the right handoff into *"Notice what is missing from that sentence."* Make the question carry something the title has not already asked. The ownership clause from item 3 does this for free: if the hook has named the assumption as *the software, or something IT owns*, then the closing question can ask **whose job it is** rather than merely **what it is** — which is a question the viewer cannot answer, which is what an open loop is for, and which is the question the body actually spends 454 words on in Part 3.

That single adjustment strengthens the loop, raises the hook's altitude, tightens the Part 1 callback, and pulls the hook towards the "Nobody Owns" half of the title. It is the highest-leverage change available in this script.
