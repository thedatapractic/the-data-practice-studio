## On the overrun, and on stakeholder engagement

**My view: the overrun is earned, and the stakeholder engagement passage is why.** It was the right call to add it.

The case for it is not that it is a nice extra. It is that **the first move fails without it.** Naming a data owner is the single most common data governance intervention and the single most common one to achieve nothing, because ownership assigned on paper to someone who was never brought along changes no behaviour at all. A video that told viewers to name an owner and stopped there would be giving advice that mostly does not work. The passage also lands in Aji's own territory: winning over people who do not report to you, by reducing their workload and crediting them publicly, is drawn from real experience rather than theory, and it is the sort of thing no generic explainer includes.

I would keep it inside move one rather than promoting it to a fourth move. Three moves is a structure the viewer can hold; four is a list they will forget. It also sits naturally there, because it is the answer to the obvious objection that arises the moment ownership is mentioned.

The remaining sixty seconds over target are spread across Part 1 and Part 3, and I do not see a passage I would strip. Part 2 has come down to 195 words and is now the leanest section in the script, which is the right shape: the "why" is felt quickly and the "how" gets the room.

## Post-review: Torch's domain-language pass (v5)

Torch reworked the draft against the new `domain-knowledge.md`. Assessed separately because it changes the domain-expertise item and the runtime.

**Domain expertise — PASS, and materially stronger than v4.** Six changes, all of which a practitioner would notice and a generalist would not have made:

1. The definition now says **"throughout its lifecycle, from the moment it is created to the day it is properly destroyed"**. Lifecycle is the field's own term, and naming destruction as a real stage is something most explainers omit entirely.
2. A new passage names the **valid versus accurate** distinction: the vaccination dates passed every format rule and range check and were still wrong. This is the single best addition in the pass. It converts the story from "context matters" into a precise diagnosis, and it is the distinction practitioners live with daily.
3. The missing thing is now identified as **business metadata** specifically, not metadata in general.
4. Part 3 now separates **data owner** from **data steward**, with the reason it matters: organisations that conflate them believe they have ownership when they have a name on a slide.
5. Writing definitions down is now named as building a **business glossary**, which gives the viewer the artefact, not just the activity.
6. The ethics passage now says **personal data**, which is the correct term and carries the regulatory weight.

The original slip is corrected: "the most expensive way to **manage data**", with "put the **validation** where the data is created".

**Runtime — 1,398 words, 9.99 minutes. At the ceiling, with no margin.** Torch already stripped two non-essential sentences to get here. I would not sign this off as comfortable: any slower delivery on the day puts it over ten minutes, and there is nothing left to cut that would not damage the script.

**My recommendation: take one distinction out and give it its own video.** Both the valid-versus-accurate passage and the owner-versus-steward passage are excellent, and both are arguably deeper than a "what is data management" introduction needs. Removing either buys roughly twenty seconds of margin. If forced to choose I would lift **owner versus steward** into a dedicated video on data roles, because it is a topic with real search demand in its own right, and keep valid-versus-accurate here because it completes the vaccination story rather than sitting alongside it.

This is an editorial judgement, not a rule, so it is Aji's call.
